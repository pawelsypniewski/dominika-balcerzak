About me

In recent years, it is not only us who live longer. So do our pets. The progress of veterinary medicine has been remarkable.

Yet I still often hear: “My dog is too old for treatment” or “The cat behaves like that because it’s getting old”. In fact, many changes in a patient’s behaviour or condition are caused not by age itself, but by a disease that may develop unnoticed for a long time.

Modern veterinary medicine offers increasingly effective diagnostics and safer treatments, regardless of your pet’s age. New therapies and specialisations help us take better care of our dogs and cats. Older patients, however, are often passed from one specialist to the next — they receive many recommendations, but no one looks at their health as a whole and guides them through the second half of life.

Who am I?

I am a veterinarian, a specialist in canine and feline diseases, and a graduate of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine at the Warsaw University of Life Sciences (SGGW), where I qualified in 2009.

Since 2017 I have been a co-owner of the Kochanowskiego Veterinary Clinic in Warsaw’s Bielany district, and since 2025 also a co-owner of its branch in Bródno. In my career I have also worked in the pharmaceutical industry and at the ALAB Weterynaria diagnostic laboratory.

For many years I worked closely with veterinary oncologists, which gave me extensive experience in caring for chronically ill and geriatric patients. My interests also include neurology, endocrinology and gastroenterology, which lets me look at each patient more broadly and connect symptoms from different areas of their health.

In my work, a holistic approach matters most to me — organising diagnostics, bringing together the recommendations of different specialists, and creating a treatment plan tailored to the age, health and circumstances of each pet and its owner.

I am not afraid of difficult patients either — including aggressive cats. I know how important the right approach, patience and respect for your pet’s boundaries are.

My priority is always the patient’s quality of life and wellbeing, and helping them function as well as possible at every stage of illness and ageing.

When
to visit

Most diseases of later life begin with a small change that is easy to put down to old age. If you notice any of these signs in your pet, it is worth having them checked.

  • Drinks noticeably more waterNeeds to urinate more often, sometimes wakes up at night to go out, and the cat starts going outside the litter tray.
  • Loses weight despite a good appetiteEats as much as usual or more, yet body weight is falling.
  • Struggles to get up and has stopped jumpingMoves stiffly after rest, climbs stairs more slowly, is reluctant to jump into the car. The cat no longer gets onto the windowsill, or chooses lower spots than before.
  • Behaviour has changedHides, is irritable, wanders around at night or cries out, sleeps all day and no longer seeks contact with the household.
  • Hears or sees less wellDoesn’t respond when called, bumps into things, gets lost in familiar places.
  • Bad breathEats more slowly and carefully, drops bits of food, drools.
  • Changes on the skinA new lump under the skin, or a change that has grown over the past few weeks.
  • Tires more quicklyShorter walks, coughing, faster breathing even at rest.
  • Coat in poorer conditionThe cat has stopped grooming; dandruff, thinning fur or bald patches have appeared.

None of these signs means illness on its own, and this list does not replace an examination — it is meant to help you notice when a visit is worthwhile. If symptoms get worse from day to day, or your pet has stopped eating and drinking, do not wait for a routine appointment — call the clinic.
